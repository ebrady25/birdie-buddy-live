Week 8 what we learned: the Burrow stack we called won the slate

Tue Nov 3, 2026 · Sunday main slate recap · BirdieBuddy NFL

Week 8 was decided in one game, and it's the one we pointed to all week. On Wednesday we called Joe Burrow, Ja'Marr Chase and Tee Higgins "the quieter big stack," with Burrow at 5% projected ownership. On Friday we said to get different with Burrow, Chase, Higgins and Tony Pollard. Tennessee and Cincinnati then combined for 79 points against a 45.5 total, and that stack became the slate.

TL;DR

  • Burrow, Chase and Higgins scored 120.1 points together. The stack was in 79% of top-1% lineups in the biggest GPPs, against 2.8% of the field.
  • Our full Friday build, Burrow, Chase, Higgins and Tony Pollard, won a 118,758-entry GPP outright and was in 34% of the top 0.1%, from 0.3% of the field.
  • The Zay Flowers fade was right: the slate's most popular player (34% owned) scored 0.0. The Puka Nacua pivot scored 25.0.
  • Wan'Dale Robinson, our $4,200 pivot off Omar Cooper Jr. (0.0 points), was in 45% of top-1% lineups.

What won

The three largest Sunday GPPs (119,000 to 198,000 entries) were won with 259.1, 254.6 and 248.4 points, and all three winners started Burrow, Chase and Higgins. Burrow was the quarterback in 97% of top-1% lineups.

Where we were right

  • The Burrow stack. It projected for a 56.9-point mean and an 84.0-point ceiling on Friday, clearing 75 in 19% of our sims, at a fraction of the Dallas stack's ownership. It scored 120.1, more than its 99th-percentile outcome (109.3). Burrow finished 9.6% owned in large fields, so the crowd caught on a little, but not enough: the full stack was in under 3% of lineups.
  • The bring-back. We kept Pollard as the cheap bring-back both days because Cincinnati "has to build its lead through the air." The game went further than that: Tennessee kept scoring, and Pollard posted 31.7 points at 4% owned. Burrow plus both receivers plus Pollard was in 0.3% of the field and 34% of the top 0.1%.
  • The Flowers fade. Flowers was our top fade on Wednesday and Friday and the slate's most popular player. He finished 34% owned in large fields and 44% in single entry, and scored 0.0. He was in 3% of top-1% lineups. The salary we said to move to Nacua bought 25.0 points.
  • The Cooper fade and the Robinson pivot. Omar Cooper Jr. was "newly chalk" at 17% with a 6% optimal rate, and he scored 0.0. Our pivot, Wan'Dale Robinson at $4,200, scored 16.7 and was in 45% of top-1% lineups, three times his 15% field rate.
  • The smaller calls. Our Friday matchup notes knocked Drake London (1.8 points) and backed Kyler Murray (20.9 at 5% owned). Darnell Washington, a $2,900 punt at 1.7% owned, scored 12.4. We faded Jordan Mason's single-entry chalk, and he scored 2.0. We also passed on the 21%-owned Broncos defense, which scored 2.0.

Who overperformed

  • Tee Higgins (40.5, projected 15.5) and Joe Burrow (45.5, projected 23.0) were the two biggest beats among popular players, and both came in the stack we called.
  • Tony Pollard (31.7, projected 10.6) and Cam Ward (30.3 at $4,600 and under 1% owned) made Tennessee's side of the shootout pay.
  • Tyler Allgeier (39.1 at 0.1% owned) and Adonai Mitchell (30.2 at $4,000) were the slate's two lowest-owned spikes. No model was on them; both were under 1.5% projected.
  • Bhayshul Tuten (32.9) ran in Jacksonville's 42–7 rout, the blowout the market gave a 40%-plus chance.
  • Harold Fannin Jr. (27.4 at $4,200, 1.9% owned) was in two of the three GPP winners.

Who underperformed

  • Zay Flowers (0.0 at 34% owned) and Omar Cooper Jr. (0.0 at 17%): two of the field's most popular receivers combined for zero, and we faded both.
  • CeeDee Lamb (12.8 at 23% owned) and George Pickens (6.8): Arizona won in Dallas 33–30, but the Cardinals' points went through Allgeier and Michael Wilson (29.8), not Jacoby Brissett (4.5).
  • Bijan Robinson (7.6) and Drake London (1.8): Atlanta was shut out 16–0 in Tampa Bay.
  • Rhamondre Stevenson (12.0 at 35% owned) and Hunter Henry (6.0 at 21%): they became Sunday chalk on New England's late news and didn't pay it off.

Where we missed

  • Dallas. The Prescott, Lamb and Pickens stack was our No. 1 build, and it scored 39.4 against a 58.5-point mean. It wasn't in a single top-1% lineup in the three biggest GPPs. We called it crowded and pointed you to Cincinnati to get different; that was the half of the advice that won.
  • Tuten. We faded him on Wednesday for Jaylen Warren (24.3), and he scored 32.9. By Friday our matchup model backed his chalk, but the Wednesday fade was wrong.
  • Late-news ownership. Value backs climbed past our projected ownership: Stevenson went from 10% projected to 35% actual, Braelon Allen from 5% to 21%, and Jonah Coleman from under 1% to 19%. We flagged this blind spot Friday ("our ownership model tends to under-call news-driven value"), and Sunday's numbers proved it. When late news hits, read our Sunday-morning update before lock.

What we're taking into Week 9

  • The quarterback plus two catchers won. 85% of top-1% lineups in the big GPPs paired the QB with at least two of his own pass catchers, against 34% of the field. In single entry it was 86% against 28%.
  • Bring it back. Top-1% lineups averaged about one bring-back player each (1.06), nearly twice the field (0.57).
  • Be different in one game, not every slot. Top-1% lineups weren't contrarian everywhere. They carried more total ownership than the field (125% vs 116%) and fewer sub-5% punts (1.35 vs 1.70). They were simply right about one game, and they drew from fewer games (5.1 vs 5.8).

Bottom line

The Week 8 playbook was the one we published: play the slate's best environment knowingly, get different in Cincinnati with Burrow, Chase, Higgins and a Pollard bring-back, and fade Zay Flowers. The stack won all three of the biggest GPPs, and the full four-man build won one outright. Dallas didn't deliver, and late-news value backs got more popular than we projected, so the Sunday update matters. The Week 9 first look is out Wednesday.
