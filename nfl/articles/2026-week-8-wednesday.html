Week 8 first look: Dallas is the stack, and everyone knows it

Dallas owns the slate's best stack and its highest team total. We build there, then get different in Cincinnati and Jacksonville.

Wed Oct 28, 2026 · BirdieBuddy NFL · numbers as of 2026-10-28 20:00 UTC

Dallas has a 30.8-point team total in a 54.5-point game, and Dak Prescott with CeeDee Lamb and George Pickens clears 75 DK points in 20% of our 10,000 sims, the best trio on the slate. The field will get there first: Lamb projects as the most popular receiver at 25% owned, and Prescott as the top QB at 15%. We build through Dallas anyway, then get different in Cincinnati and Jacksonville.

TL;DR

  • Build through Dallas: Prescott, Lamb and Pickens clear 75 DK points in 20% of our sims, the best rate on the slate.
  • Get different with Burrow, Chase and Higgins; Burrow projects at only 5% owned.
  • Eat the Bijan Robinson chalk: he lands in our sims' best lineup about as often as his 21% projected ownership.
  • Fade Jahmyr Gibbs and pivot to Jaylen Warren, projected at 9% owned.

Games to attack

  • ARI@DAL (54.5, the No. 1 game by our skill-player projection): our game sims give Dallas a 30% chance to win by 14+ and Arizona a 19% chance to win by 4+. Stack Dallas and bring it back with one Arizona pass-catcher.
  • BAL@BUF (54.3, No. 2): most likely close, with a 28% market chance the margin is 3 or less. Its best stack clears 75 in only 6% of sims, so play it one-off: Josh Allen has a 37.7-point ceiling at 9% owned.
  • TEN@CIN (46.5, No. 4): our game sims give Cincinnati a 42% chance to win by 14+. Stack the Bengals and bring it back with a cheap Titans receiver.

Stacks to build

1. Dak Prescott + CeeDee Lamb + George Pickens (DAL)

  • Numbers: mean 58.1, ceiling 85.8, clears 75 in 20% of 10,000 sims, $21,000. Projected at 15%, 25% and 13% owned.
  • Bring-back + game flow: Trey McBride lifts the four-player ceiling to 108.5. Dallas has to keep throwing in a shootout rather than sit on an early lead.
  • Why it wins a GPP: it's the best stack on the slate, and the field knows it, so it will be crowded. To get off the crowded version, swap Pickens for Ryan Flournoy (4% owned): still 13% to clear 75, at $19,000.

2. Joe Burrow + Ja'Marr Chase + Tee Higgins (CIN)

  • Numbers: mean 56.0, ceiling 82.9, 17% to clear 75, $21,400. Projected at 5%, 18% and 10% owned.
  • Bring-back + game flow: Carnell Tate (5% owned) pushes the four-player ceiling to 97.4. Cincinnati needs to score through the air before a big lead hands the game to the run.
  • Why it wins a GPP: close to Dallas's upside with a QB at a third of Prescott's ownership. The risk is the blowout: Chase Brown (22% owned) takes over the fourth quarter.

3. Trevor Lawrence + Parker Washington + Brenton Strange (JAX)

  • Numbers: mean 48.4, ceiling 72.9, 9% to clear 75, $16,700. Projected at 6%, 14% and 10% owned.
  • Bring-back + game flow: Jonathan Taylor (4% owned) gets the four-player ceiling to 87.7. Jacksonville is a 9.8-point favorite; the stack hits if Indianapolis keeps it close enough that Lawrence keeps throwing.
  • Why it wins a GPP: Lawrence is the cheapest way into a 28.2-point team total, and the savings pay for two stud RBs. The risk: a 39% market chance of a 14-point Jaguars win that runs through Bhayshul Tuten.

The codex says

  • 50% of 169 Milly winners (k = 85) came from a game totaled 48 or higher, vs 27% of 2,639 games. Only ARI@DAL and BAL@BUF clear that bar this week.
  • 25% of winners (k = 43) stacked a QB whose team was favored by 7+, vs 14% of 5,278 games. Prescott, Burrow and Lawrence all play for 7-point favorites or bigger.

Matchups to exploit

Our matchup model is weak this early (reliability under .25 at every position), so treat these as tie-breakers, not reasons.

  • Bhayshul Tuten: Indianapolis has allowed the second-most DK points to lead backs, an edge of +0.5 points per game over 5 games.
  • Kyler Murray: Detroit has allowed the most QB points (30.2 per game over 5 games), an edge of +0.8. He's $5,600 and 2% owned.
  • Trey McBride: Dallas has held lead tight ends to 3.1 DK points per game over 5 games (edge −0.35).

Punts

  • Broncos DST ($2,300): a 14.0-point ceiling against a Kansas City team implied for 20.7. At 14% projected, they're the chalk defense, and the price justifies it.
  • Colby Parkinson (TE, $3,100): a 16.6-point ceiling at 3% owned, and he lands in our sims' best lineup more often than he's owned. Cheap value like this is where our ownership model tends to come in low, so expect him to be more popular than 3%.
  • Kendrick Bourne (WR, $3,900): a 16.9-point ceiling at 5% owned, and a cheap Arizona bring-back for Dallas stacks.

Chalk we'll eat / chalk we'll fade

Eat

  • Bijan Robinson ($8,500, 21% owned): 35.1-point ceiling, and he lands in our sims' best lineup 21% of the time, right at his ownership. Nearly Gibbs's ceiling for less salary.
  • Chase Brown ($6,300, 22% owned): 29.7-point ceiling and the best RB value on the slate at 2.85 points per $1K. A Bengals blowout is exactly his game.

Fade

  • Jahmyr Gibbs ($9,500, 19% owned): the top RB ceiling at 35.2, but only 2.3 points per $1K, and he makes the best lineup less often (16%) than the field plays him. Pivot to Jaylen Warren ($6,600, 9% owned, in the best lineup 13% of the time). Our model can come in low on value backs like Warren, so his ownership may climb.
  • Trey McBride ($7,400, 21% owned): his 16% best-lineup rate trails his ownership, and Dallas has shut down tight ends. Pivot to Hunter Henry ($4,300, 5% owned, 7% best-lineup rate).

How we build these numbers

  • Lines and game flows: de-vigged prediction-market prices (Polymarket + Kalshi), fit to spread, total and team-total ladders. Game-flow chances come from the market's ±3.5 / ±13.5 lines where they trade.
  • Projections and ceilings: our drive-by-drive game simulator, built on nflverse play-by-play 2016–26, anchored to the market totals and player props, with public injury and role news. 10,000 simulated games per matchup. A stack's numbers come from the same simulated games, so the correlation is built in.
  • Ownership: our own model, trained on DraftKings contest results, shown as projected % for large-field GPPs (and single-entry contests where they differ) plus tiers by rank within position. It ranks players well, but its levels are rough, and it under-calls late, news-driven value.
  • Matchups: points allowed to each role by each defense, opponent-adjusted and shrunk toward zero. One season of this is mostly noise, so it only breaks ties.
  • The codex: 169 DraftKings Milly Maker winning lineups (2016–25) against 2,639 games. Winner rates are shown with k and n.
