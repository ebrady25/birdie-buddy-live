Week 8 first look: Dallas's 30.9-point total and the crowd chasing it

Dallas is the slate's best stack and the field knows it; Burrow and Lawrence offer quieter, cheaper ways to win Week 8.

Wed Oct 28, 2026 · BirdieBuddy NFL · numbers as of 2026-10-28 23:30 UTC

Dallas carries a 30.9-point team total, the biggest on the main slate, and its top stack clears 75 DK points in 21% of our 10,000 sims. The field sees it too: CeeDee Lamb projects at 33% owned and Dak Prescott at 26%, both higher in single entry. We build Dallas, then look for quieter routes around it.

TL;DR

  • Stack Prescott, Lamb and Pickens: the slate's best stack clears 75 points in 21% of sims, but expect a crowd.
  • Burrow, Higgins and Chase is the quieter big stack, with Burrow at just 5% projected ownership.
  • Lawrence, Parker Washington and Strange cost $16,700, cheap enough to add Bijan Robinson and his 20.5% optimal rate.
  • Fade Bhayshul Tuten at 20% projected ownership; Jaylen Warren has the higher ceiling for $700 more.

Two games clear 54: ARI@DAL at 54.5 and BAL@BUF at 54.3. Nothing else on the main slate tops 46.5, but two double-digit favorites, Cincinnati (28.7) and Jacksonville (28.2), carry the next-best team totals. KC@DEN (40.7) and CLE@PIT (39.1) are the games to skip for stacks.

Games to attack

  • ARI@DAL (54.5, No. 1 environment): the market gives Dallas a 31% blowout chance and another 31% to win by 4–13. Stack Dallas and bring it back with Arizona.
  • BAL@BUF (54.3, No. 2): Lamar Jackson is at a 25% chance to play as of Wednesday and needs news, so Tyler Huntley likely starts. The flows split evenly, 27% each for Close, Fav wins and Upset. Play one-offs, or Huntley: a 29.1-point ceiling at 1% projected owned.
  • TEN@CIN (46.5, No. 3): market: 42% Cincinnati wins by 14+, with Tennessee implied for 17.9. Stack the Bengals and keep the bring-back to Tony Pollard.

Stacks to build

1. Dak Prescott + CeeDee Lamb + George Pickens (DAL)

  • Numbers: 58.5 mean, 86.1 ceiling, 21% to clear 75 in 10,000 sims, $21,000. Projected owned: Prescott 26%, Lamb 33%, Pickens 11%.
  • Bring-back + game flow: Trey McBride ($7,400) pushes the four-player ceiling to 109.0. It hits when Dallas wins comfortably and Arizona keeps throwing.
  • Why it wins a GPP: it's the best stack on the slate, and the field knows it: crowded. Swap Pickens for Ryan Flournoy (4%) to save $2,000 and still clear 75 in 13.7% of sims. Risk: Javonte Williams scores the touchdowns.

2. Joe Burrow + Tee Higgins + Ja'Marr Chase (CIN)

  • Numbers: 54.5 mean, 81.0 ceiling, 15.3% to clear 75, $21,400. Projected owned: Burrow 5%, Higgins 13%, Chase 14%.
  • Bring-back + game flow: Tony Pollard (2%) lifts the four-player ceiling to 94.2. Cincinnati has to build its lead through the air rather than sit on it.
  • Why it wins a GPP: Burrow costs $700 more than Prescott at a fraction of his ownership. Risk: Chase is questionable (80% to play as of Wednesday); if he sits, pivot to Higgins and Mike Gesicki.

3. Trevor Lawrence + Parker Washington + Brenton Strange (JAX)

  • Numbers: 49.1 mean, 74.0 ceiling, 9.3% to clear 75, $16,700, the best points per $1K (2.94) among our top stacks. Projected owned: Lawrence 7%, Washington 11%, Strange 15%.
  • Bring-back + game flow: Jonathan Taylor (4%) makes it an 89.8-point four-player ceiling. Market: 40% Jacksonville wins by 14+; the stack needs those points through the air, not from Bhayshul Tuten.
  • Why it wins a GPP: the low price buys Bijan Robinson ($8,500), whose 20.5% optimal rate leads the slate at 9% projected owned. Risk: Strange is chalk at tight end.

The codex says

  • Only ARI@DAL and BAL@BUF top 48. In our study of 169 Milly Maker winners, 50% (k = 85) stacked a game totaled 48 or more, vs 27% of 2,639 games.
  • All three stack QBs are 7+ point favorites: 25% of winners (k = 43) had a QB favored by 7+, vs 14% of 5,278 team-games. Just 44% of winners (k = 75) used a bring-back, so the run-back is optional.

Matchups to exploit

Our matchup model is weak at this point in the season (reliability under .25 at every role, about .06 for WR1s), so treat these as tie-breakers, not reasons.

  • Kyler Murray gets Detroit, which has allowed the most QB points (30.2 DK per game over 5 games); edge +0.78 per game, at 1% projected owned.
  • Bhayshul Tuten draws an Indianapolis defense second in RB1 points allowed, +0.52 per game over 5 games.
  • Trey McBride faces a Dallas defense allowing the fewest TE points, 3.1 DK per game over 5 games (edge −0.35). Tampa Bay has held WR1s to 3.8 DK per game over 5 games, a knock on Drake London.

Chalk we'll eat / chalk we'll fade

Eat

  • Javonte Williams ($7,300, 25% projected owned): a 31.3-point ceiling as the lead back in the slate's best team total, optimal in 12.3% of sims.
  • Brenton Strange ($4,000, 15%): a 22.1-point ceiling and the best TE value at 2.89 points per $1K.

Fade

  • Bhayshul Tuten ($5,900, 20%): optimal in just 8.7% of sims with a 24.0 ceiling. Pivot to Jaylen Warren ($6,600, 8%, 12.9% optimal, 28.3 ceiling). Value plays like Warren are where our ownership model tends to run low, so his number may climb.
  • Zay Flowers ($7,200, 26%; 29% in single entry): with Jackson unlikely to play, he's tied to Huntley. Spend up to Bijan Robinson (9%) instead.

Omar Cooper Jr. plays chalkier in single-entry contests: 19% there vs 11% in large fields.

How we build these numbers

  • Lines and game flows: de-vigged prediction-market prices (Polymarket + Kalshi), fit to spread, total and team-total ladders. Game-flow chances come from the market's ±3.5 / ±13.5 lines where they trade.
  • Projections and ceilings: our drive-by-drive game simulator, built on nflverse play-by-play 2016–26, anchored to the market totals and player props, with public injury and role news. 10,000 simulated games per matchup. A stack's numbers come from the same simulated games, so the correlation is built in.
  • Ownership: our own model, trained on DraftKings contest results, shown as projected % for large-field GPPs (and single-entry contests where they differ) plus tiers by rank within position. It ranks players well, but its levels are rough, and it under-calls late, news-driven value.
  • Matchups: points allowed to each role by each defense, opponent-adjusted and shrunk toward zero. One season of this is mostly noise, so it only breaks ties.
  • The codex: 169 DraftKings Milly Maker winning lineups (2016–25) against 2,639 games. Winner rates are shown with k and n.
