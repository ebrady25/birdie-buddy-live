Week 9 what we learned: our Green Bay stack carried the top 1%

Mon Nov 9, 2026 · Sunday main slate recap · BirdieBuddy NFL

The top lineups in Week 9 ran through the game we told you to get different in. On Friday we wrote, "Get different in Green Bay, where Love sits at 2% in a game with no chalk." The Packers won 45–27 at New England as 3-point underdogs, and Love, Golden and Watson combined for 107.7 points.

TL;DR

  • Love, Golden and Watson scored 107.7 points together. The stack was in 31% of top-1% lineups in the 198,012-entry GPP, against 0.8% of the field.
  • Two of the three biggest GPP winners started the Green Bay trio, and Love was the quarterback in 73% of that GPP's top 0.1%.
  • The chalk we ate paid: CeeDee Lamb (37.5) and Puka Nacua (39.0) were both in the biggest GPP's winning lineup. Kenneth Walker III, our Kansas City one-off, scored 39.3.
  • The QB fade was right: Malik Willis scored 4.8 at 13% owned, and Bo Nix, our pivot, scored 16.7 at 1.9%. Jahmyr Gibbs, our RB fade, scored 13.4 at 28%.

What won

The three largest Sunday GPPs (19,009 to 198,012 entries) were won with 223.2, 221.4 and 205.3 points. It took about 173 points to reach the top 1% in all three.

Where we were right

  • The Green Bay stack. On Friday it projected for a 44.1-point mean and a 69.4-point ceiling, the third of our three stacks. It scored 107.7, above its 99th-percentile outcome (95.1). The field barely touched it: 0.8%, 0.7% and 0.6% of lineups in the three biggest GPPs. The top 1% had it in 31%, 30% and 28%, and the top 0.1% in 49%, 56% and 32%.
  • The Stevenson bring-back. Rhamondre Stevenson scored 15.8 against a 15.2 projection. The full four-man stack was in 0.3% of the field in each of the two biggest GPPs, and in 13% of their top 1% and 18% of their top 0.1%.
  • The chalk we ate. Lamb scored 37.5 (projected 20.3) and Nacua 39.0 (projected 21.9). Across the three GPPs, Lamb was in 33% of top-1% lineups against 19% of the field, and Nacua in 42% against 28%. Jonah Coleman, our Friday eat at $5,300, scored 18.8 and was in 58% of top-1% lineups against 39% of the field.
  • The fades. Willis was "optimal in just 5% of sims" at 16% projected. He finished 13% owned, scored 4.8, and was in 0.5% of top-1% lineups. Nix, who we said "matches his 27.6-point ceiling," scored 16.7. Gibbs ($10,000) scored 13.4 against a 24.4 projection at 28% owned and showed up in 2.6% of top-1% lineups, against 27% of the field.
  • Walker in Kansas City. Friday: "a 40% Kansas City blowout chance points to Kenneth Walker III and his 34.4-point ceiling." The Chiefs won 35–12, and Walker scored 39.3, past that ceiling, at 12% owned. He was in 32% of the biggest GPP's top 1%, against 12% of its field.
  • DeMario Douglas. We named him ($4,800, 5%) as a player who would "carry more of the New England offense." He scored 18.2 at 7.7% owned and was in two of the three winning lineups.

Who overperformed

  • Jordan Love (39.2, projected 17.0), Matthew Golden (35.4, projected 14.0) and Christian Watson (33.1, projected 13.1) each beat their projection by about 20 points, all in the stack we called.
  • C.J. Stroud (39.4 at 2.6% owned, projected 17.0) led Houston's 44–22 win at the Chargers, a game totaled 40.5. Kayshon Boutte (31.5 at $3,400 and 0.1% owned) and Tank Dell (23.5 at $3,500 and 0.8% owned) caught the passes. We had both under 5 projected points.
  • Darren Waller (23.0 at $3,300 and 0.8% owned, projected 5.2) returned 6.97 points per $1K and was in two of the three winners.
  • Shedeur Sanders (22.7 at $4,000 and 5.1% owned) was the quarterback in 19% of the biggest GPP's top-1% lineups. Our Friday update had moved his projection from 2.7 to 13.1 once he became the likely starter.
  • Jordan Watkins (18.6 at $3,300 and 3.3% owned) was in the 19,009-entry winner.

Who underperformed

  • Jahmyr Gibbs (13.4 at 28%) and Malik Willis (4.8 at 13%): Detroit won 19–9 in Miami, and two of the field's most popular players didn't come close to paying off. We faded both.
  • Greg Dulcich (3.4 at 22% owned): our fade on Wednesday and Friday, and he scored 3.4. At $3,100, though, he was still in 20% of top-1% lineups, close to his field rate, and in the 147,000-entry winner. The fade saved points but didn't create much of an edge.
  • George Kittle (9.8 at 27%) and Jaxon Smith-Njigba (12.1 at 20%, projected 20.4) were two of the highest-owned pass catchers, and neither paid it off.
  • Devin Singletary (5.9 at 27% owned) and Antonio Williams (5.5 at 23%) became late-news chalk, and both busted. Drew Lock was the field's most-played quarterback at 14% in the biggest GPP and scored 12.6.

Where we missed

  • Dallas. Our No. 1 stack didn't land. George Pickens scored 0.0, and Ryan Flournoy, the replacement we named, scored 7.3 at 19% owned. Prescott (23.5) and Lamb (37.5) did their part, but the trio with Flournoy finished at 68.3, under the 75-point line, and Jonathan Taylor scored 10.4 against a 15.6 projection. Lamb alone was the right way in.
  • Detroit, Kittle and Judkins. Jared Goff scored 6.3 against a 20.3 projection, and our Friday Detroit stack wasn't in a single top-1% lineup. George Kittle, our Wednesday eat, scored 9.8. Quinshon Judkins, our pivot off Gibbs, scored 5.5.
  • Late-news ownership, again. Singletary went from 3.1% projected to 27% actual, Flournoy from 3.7% to 19%, Coleman from 23% to 36%, and Lock from 3.5% to 14%. This is the second week running. When news breaks Sunday, read our Sunday-morning update before lock.

What we're taking into Week 10

  • The QB plus two catchers won again. 53% of top-1% lineups in the big GPPs paired the quarterback with at least two of his own pass catchers, against 35% of the field. In single entry it was 53% against 28%.
  • The winning QB was low-owned. Top-1% lineups rostered a quarterback averaging 4.3% ownership, against 7.3% for the field (9.4% in single entry). Love (3.0%) and Stroud (2.6%) made the difference.
  • This week rewarded being different. Unlike Week 8, top-1% lineups carried less total ownership than the field (117% vs 125%) and more sub-5% players (2.4 vs 1.8). Cheap spikes like Waller and Dell did that work.
  • Bring-backs were a small edge. Top-1% lineups averaged 0.59 bring-back players against 0.54 for the field, and 22% used an RB bring-back against 18%.

Bottom line

Week 9 was won in the game we pointed to as the contrarian play: Love, Golden and Watson at under 1% of the field, with a Stevenson bring-back, in a 45–27 Packers win. The chalk we ate, Lamb, Nacua and Coleman, paid, and the Willis and Gibbs fades kept lineups away from two of the slate's most popular busts. Dallas and Detroit missed, and late-news ownership got away from our numbers for a second week, so check the Sunday update. The Week 10 first look is out Wednesday.
