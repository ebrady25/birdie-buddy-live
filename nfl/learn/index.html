How to use BirdieBuddy NFL in 5 minutes

Every week we publish our own projections, ownership and lineup ideas for DraftKings, in plain English. You don't need to read all of it. Here's the short path, then a glossary for any word you don't know.

Your week, in seven steps

  1. Wednesday eveningRead the first look. One article with the stacks we like, the games to attack and the chalk we'd fade. Articles →
  2. Any timeOpen This Week. Start with the box at the top: one stack, one value, one fade. Everything under it is the detail behind those picks. This Week →
  3. BuildingCheck Projections for any player: our points, his likely range, how popular he'll be and whether he's worth his salary. Projections →
  4. Primetime gamesShort on time? Read Chill. Five minutes: the likely game script, three captains, the chalk and three lineups. Want everything? Grind is the full playbook. Next showdown →

  6. Sunday noonCheck Before You Lock. Late injuries and inactives, and who gains from them, an hour before the early games. Before You Lock →

Glossary

Projection
How many DraftKings points we expect a player to score.Our average outcome across thousands of simulated games.
Range
A bad day to a big day: the low and high end of what he usually scores. Shown as P10–P90 on some tables (1-in-10 bad, 1-in-10 great).
Ceiling
His big-day score. Tournaments are won by ceilings, not averages.
Value
Points per $1,000 of salary. 3x means 3 points for every $1,000.
Ownership
The share of lineups in a contest that use a player. 30% means about 3 in every 10 lineups.We show it two ways: big tournaments (GPPs) and single-entry contests, because the crowds pick differently.
Chalk
The most popular players of the week. Chalk isn't bad, but if everyone has him, he doesn't help you pass anyone.
Fade
Leaving a popular player out on purpose, because we think the crowd is overrating him.
Pivot
A similar player at a similar price that far fewer people will play.
Optimal %
How often a player shows up in the best possible lineup across our simulations. "In the best lineup."
Leverage
Optimal % minus ownership: how much the crowd is underrating (positive) or overrating (negative) a player. "Edge vs the crowd."
Stack
A quarterback plus his own pass catchers in the same lineup, so their points rise and fall together.
Bring-back
A player from the other team in your stack's game, for when the game turns into a shootout.
Boom rate
How often a stack scores 75+ DraftKings points together in our simulations (shown as ≥75).
Implied team total
How many real points a team is expected to score, worked out from the betting spread and total. Higher totals usually mean more fantasy points.
Game script
How a game is likely to unfold: a blowout, a close game or a shootout. It changes who gets the ball.
GPP
A guaranteed prize pool tournament: big field, top-heavy payouts. You need upside and some unique picks.
Single entry
A contest where each person gets one lineup. Fields are smaller and sharper, so we show their ownership separately.
Showdown
A one-game DraftKings contest: a captain plus five flex players from the two teams.
Captain
In Showdown, your captain scores 1.5x his points and costs 1.5x his salary.
Dupes
Other people with the exact same lineup. If you win with a duplicated lineup, you split the prize.
Top 1%
Finishing in the top 1 of every 100 entries: where most tournament prize money is.
BirdieBuild
Our study of past winning DraftKings lineups, and the rules we built from it. The BirdieBuild score (0–100) in the Lab says how closely your lineup is built like past winners.
