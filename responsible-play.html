Responsible play

Keep it fun.

Daily fantasy sports should be a hobby you enjoy, not a source of stress. Here's how to keep it that way, and where to get help if it's starting not to be.

What BirdieBuddy is, and isn't

  • We publish projections, ownership estimates, playbooks and tools. We don't run contests, take entries or hold your money.
  • Our numbers are for information and entertainment. They're our best estimates, not guarantees, and they're not wagering advice. Even a great process loses most weeks in big tournaments; that's how they work.
  • You must be 18 or older to use BirdieBuddy, and 21 or older where your state requires it for daily fantasy sports.
  • A good read on a slate is never a reason to play more than you planned.

Habits that keep it a hobby

  • Set a budget before the week starts, money you'd be fine spending on any other entertainment, and stick to it.
  • Never chase losses. A bad Sunday isn't a reason to enter more on Monday night.
  • Don't play with money meant for bills, rent, savings or anything else you need, and never borrow to play.
  • Set limits where you play. Contest sites, DraftKings included, offer tools in your account settings like deposit limits, entry limits, time-outs and self-exclusion. Use them.
  • Take breaks. Skipping a slate is always an option.
  • Keep it social and sober. Decisions made late at night, upset or after drinking tend to be the ones you regret.

A quick self-check

Be honest with yourself. If you answer "yes" to any of these, it may be time to slow down or talk to someone:

  • Do you spend more money or time on contests than you planned to?
  • Have you entered more to try to win back what you lost?
  • Do you think about contests or lineups when you'd rather be doing something else?
  • Have you hidden how much you play or spend from family or friends?
  • Have you used money meant for bills or savings, or borrowed money, to play?
  • Do you feel restless, irritable or down when you try to cut back?
  • Has playing caused problems at work or school, or in your relationships?
  • Do you play to escape stress, boredom or problems?
  • Have you tried to cut back or stop and found you couldn't?

Where to get help

  • 1-800-GAMBLER (1-800-426-2537): confidential support and referrals, 24/7.
  • National Problem Gambling Helpline from the National Council on Problem Gambling: 1-800-522-4700, plus chat and state resources at ncpgambling.org.
  • Gamblers Anonymous: meetings and support at gamblersanonymous.org.
  • Your contest site: ask them to set limits, a cool-off period or self-exclusion on your account.

If you're worried about someone else, these same helplines can help you figure out how to support them.
